# Panel geometry

**The panel window does not change size while a details card opens.** `FloatingPanelController.Layout.size(for:)` is card + pointer + gap + rail (or the top-axis equivalent). The card is an **overlay** on the rail, never a stack sibling: anything that changes the geometry the rail is laid out in animates the rail sideways although it never moves on screen.

The window **may** resize when the rail is re-docked onto the other axis (side ↔ top), under the pointer with no card open. Why: [../decisions/panel-frame.md](../decisions/panel-frame.md).

## Overlay and height

- Apply `.padding(.top, railTop)` **after** the card overlay. Before it, the card is anchored to the panel top and sliced `railTop` too high (shipped once; invisible while the rail is centred).
- **`Layout.size` is whole points** (rounded up). AppKit rounds the window frame and SwiftUI draws against it, while hit tests use `Layout.size`; at 280.44 the docked rail's hit rect stopped 0.56pt short of the screen edge and the sliver opened and shut in a loop. `RailGeometryTests`.
- The frame fits the **tallest card**, not just the rail (`max(DockLayout.maximum…, DetailCardLayout.maximumHeight)`), or the card is sliced flat against the edge.
- The rail is centred in the panel (`PanelEdge.railAlignment`). `railTop` / `railLeading` convert rail-relative card positions (may be negative) to panel space. The pointer is panel-relative.
- The window is sized for the rings **switched on** (`AppSettings.railSlotCount` → `PanelMetrics.makeRoom`), not every provider (dozens of providers made it thousands of points taller than any screen). Each path below ends in `placePanel()`; miss one and the window's rects are measured against a rail of the old length:
  - a provider switched on or off, or the shown accounts changing: `settingsChanged()`. This **does** resize the window.
  - panel size, spacing or another layout setting: `AppSettings.onLayoutChange` → `AppDelegate.layoutChanged`, which does not refresh providers ([../architecture.md](../architecture.md#settings-and-persistence)).
  - a *reading* that changes the rail's length: `PanelPlacement.railLengthChanged()` (a split account draws one ring until its first answer carries both groups). It cannot resize: the frame's size is `Layout.size(for: edge)`, a function of the edge and the shown-ring budget.
- A card opening or the pointer arriving never resizes the window. A reading changes the **rail**, not the window (`DockLayout.maximumHeight` still); a split account is budgeted for both rings from the moment it is split. Measure with `FloatingUsagePanelContent.railSize`, not the window.

**The card's content is masked to `UsageBubbleShape`.** Switching cards keeps one `UsageDetailCard` and swaps its rows; an added row appears at its final place while the outline is still growing on the spring. Only the content is masked (the surface keeps its own edge, where glass draws a rim). The mask is the safety net, not the motion:

- An added row trails the outline (`rowTransition`: opacity + 6pt drop, 0.06s delay + 0.14s); a removed row leaves in 0.06s.
- The old title leaves in 0.06s and the new one fades straight in (`headerTransition`). The header is keyed by account **and** title, so split rings swap too.
- Percentages swap outright (`.contentTransition(.identity)`); a cross-fade overlapped them and a numeric roll showed blank digits.
- **Keep all of it short.** A sweep down the rail switches cards every ~0.1s and restarts every transition; at 0.14s + 0.22s the card was mostly empty. The card's spring (`selectedSlot`) is 0.28s for the same reason.

**`UsageBubbleShape` is the card and its pointer as one path**, same winding (`sweep` when mirrored); two views in a stack drift apart when height and pointer target change together. `pointerCenter` is `animatableData`. Check both edges after any change. With `usesRoundEnds` on, the tail's flanks leave the card **along its edge** (first control point on the edge) and bend out to a ~50° tip; off, they leave at an angle and crease where they meet the card.

**On glass the surface is two shapes fused** (`LiquidBubbleSurface` in UsageDetailCard.swift): `UsageBubbleShape.only(.body)` and `.only(.tail)` as two `glassEffect`s in a `GlassEffectContainer` (spacing 12pt × scale). As one outline, glass refracted the narrow tail as all rim, like a crystal stuck on the card. On real glass the flowing tail and a single-contour outline changed nothing, and spacing 40 rippled the body's edge. Content is masked to the whole outline; the solid card fills it in one go.

**A limit's bar is continuous or segmented** (`AppSettings.usageBarStyle`, `UsageBar`, shared with the menu's dashboard). Segmented is a row of 2.5pt ticks 2pt apart, as many as fit, lit in proportion — at least one once a limit is touched, all only when it is spent (`UsageBarTests`). It is drawn 12pt tall against the continuous bar's 6, so the ticks read as ticks, and that height is in the card's budget: `DetailCardLayout.progressBarHeight` reads `PanelMetrics.usageBarStyle`, set before the change is announced, and the panel's tree is rebuilt on it (`.id`).

**An estimate is said in words, never with "≈"** (2026-10-15, at the reader's request): "Estimated value" on the limit row, the history section's footnote, the recap's provenance line and its money card. The sign beside the words said it twice; a figure with no words beside it would need them, not the sign.

On the card, the window's name has the top row to itself; spent and reset pair on the line below the bar (a shared top line fails when a limit is scoped to a model group).

### Detailed card

Switched on per account (`AppSettings.detailedCards`; [settings.md](settings.md#provider-panes)). It adds to the compact card:

- the plan beside the title and "Updated …" under it (live readings only; a stale one keeps its footnote);
- on the first account of a provider with a history (`Provider.cardHistory`): today / 7 days / 31 days as tokens, a static 31-day chart with today lit, the top model, the cache hit rate, and a line saying where the figures are from. Bars are always sized for 31 days; sized for the days on record, a history starting today became one bar as wide as the card.

**Cache hit rate** (`UsageLedger.cacheHitRate`, same 31 days as the top model) is cache reads over all input tokens (fresh, cache written, cache read; output is not input). Codex writes no cache and still has one. Left out, never 0%, where the records cannot vouch for their split: tokens no kind claims (a bare total), `hasPartialCounts`, or no input at all (so never on Z.ai's or Zhipu's statistics). `UsageLedger.adding` sums the agents' kinds so a merged provider can have one. `CacheHitRateTests`.

**Prompt cache** (Claude Code and Codex; `ClaudePromptCache`, `CodexPromptCache`, `PromptCacheLapse`; `PromptCacheLapseTests`, `CodexPromptCacheTests`):

- **Card.** "Prompt cache (1 hr) · 38 min left", or "Expired", gone a day later. Every main session is timed; the card names the **soonest to lapse** ("Prompt cache · 3 chats · Soonest in 10 min"), and with several a budgeted line (`promptCacheNameHeight`) names that conversation (rename, else opening prompt, else project; cut with "…"). With several Codex conversations the card states the floor plainly and the name line says whose it is.
- **Settings** (`PromptCacheSessionsGroup`, Claude Code's and Codex's panes) lists them all: read every 30 s, red in a session's last five minutes, named by rename, else opening prompt.
- **Claude Code** reads the tier each reply wrote (`cache_creation.ephemeral_1h_input_tokens` / `ephemeral_5m_input_tokens`); a hit renews the cache, so a session lapses at its last cache-touching request plus the tier it last wrote. No tier in the last 512 KB, no lapse.
- **The time is the request's, not the reply's**: a reply is logged when it finishes streaming (184 responses measured: median 2 s late, 99% within 80 s, max ~2 min). The start is the entry that prompted the reply (message or tool result), found by walking back through the response's blocks; outside the 512 KB tail or over 20 minutes earlier, the reply's own time stands.
- **Files.** Only files written in the last hour, plus the newest for "Expired". Subagents (a folder deeper) and sidechain lines are not read: they do not make the next main-agent message cheaper.
- **Not knowable:** a changed prefix (model, tools, system prompt, compacting, clearing) invalidates a cache that keeps counting down here; Settings says the figure assumes nothing changed.
- **Codex's is a documented floor.** The log names each turn's model (nearest `turn_context` before the request, else the session header) but not cache lifetime. OpenAI's prompt-caching guide gives GPT-5.6 and later 30 minutes after the last write or reuse, possibly longer, so the lapse is the last cache-touching request + 30 minutes, flagged `isMinimum`: "At least 18 min left", then "May have lapsed", never "Expired"; Settings calls it the guaranteed time and notes heavy traffic can miss inside it (caches are per machine). Earlier models ("typically" 5–10 min or ~30 min, by an unrecorded retention setting) are not timed (`statesLifetime(forModel:)`: `gpt-`, version ≥ 5.6). Request time and the 20-minute guard are as for Claude Code (on a real rollout the tool output preceded the usage line by 3 s). Every rollout is listed; only those written in the last half hour are opened. `codex-auto-review` is only a session setting, never a request's model.

**The history has four sources (`CardHistorySource`), and the section names its source** (`CardHistoryTests`):

- **A console's own record** (`.accountLogs`): OpenCode Go's request log and DeepSeek's daily usage, once the console sign-in is kept in Settings ([../providers/opencode-go.md](../providers/opencode-go.md), [../providers/deepseek.md](../providers/deepseek.md#the-console-usage-history-and-a-balance-without-a-key)). "Whole account", **priced as charged** (the footnote says so; the console's currency via `UsageLedger.currency`, e.g. DeepSeek's yuan; footnote names the console). Needs no Token spend; a stale session says "read it again in Settings".
- **Qoder's credits** (no `cardHistory`): `QoderCreditSection` in the same place and at the same heights, Today / 7 days / 31 days in credits used with "Charged …" under them and the month as bars, from the account's records, asked when the detailed card opens ([../providers/qoder.md](../providers/qoder.md#credit-history-issue-88)).
- **On this Mac** (`.transcripts`, `.agents`): Claude Code's and Codex's transcripts and every Token spend agent that borrows a provider's mark (Kimi CLI, Grok, OpenCode, Cursor, MiniMax Code, Command Code, Copilot; Antigravity's three and Devin's two agents summed by day, `UsageLedger.adding`). An `.agents` source can also carry **slices** — the records inside another agent's store that state this provider as their backend (`CardHistorySource.AgentSlice`), so a multi-provider client answers the card where the provider's own clients were never installed. Today only Devin has one: the `provider: "devin"` records in Oh My Pi's transcripts. A slice is a view of the store, never a second agent: it is filtered after the catalogue read, kept in its own cache file (`agent-*-omp-provider-devin.json`), and exempt from the kept-history archive, so the agent's whole ledger and the Token spend pane are untouched and nothing counts twice. The seven pre-catalogue readers carry no provider field, so slices exist only over catalogue-read agents (`SpendAgent.isLegacy`). Only with Token spend on; API-rate prices. **A `*` marks an amount that leaves out a model with no published price** (#90), as the Token spend pane marks it: an unmarked "$0.04" under 740M tokens read as the cost of all of them. **No footnote under this Mac's records**: the card once ended with "Costs are estimates at API prices." (and "API-price estimates. \* Partly unpriced." beside a `*`); the user had it removed, and the Token spend pane is where the estimate and the `*` are explained. Account-wide and charged figures keep their one line saying whose figures they are.
- **Whole account** for Z.ai and Zhipu (`.accountStatistics`): their consoles' statistics, requested with the stored key. Never priced, so no money line. A failed request says "Couldn't read the history." rather than show an empty month.

Other rules:

- **The estimated value is the one added line on a limit row** ("Estimated value $201 · $92.40 used", dimmer): `BudgetEstimate` from the card's ledger, with the settings pane's rules and withholding, only where the ledger has money and quarter-hours (this Mac's records, never Z.ai's or Zhipu's statistics), and **never for OpenCode Go or DeepSeek** (`UsageDetailCard.estimatesValue`; OpenCode states its limits in money, DeepSeek's ring is a balance). `UsageLedger.adding` keeps the agents' quarter-hours in time order for it. Budgeted as one row line per limit.
- **No clock on the bar**: a tick needed explaining; the ring's window-clock arc (`showsWindowClock`) carries that comparison.
- **The chart is static**: the panel never becomes key, so a hover readout would not fire.
- **The ledger is read off the card** (`CardLedgers`): on selection, at most every five minutes per provider (so one statistics request for Z.ai and Zhipu), or at once when the source changed (a console session read or removed), in a task not tied to the view. A sweep would cancel a view task, and a cancelled caller gets an empty ledger that would be stored as "no history".
- **Budget:** `PanelMetrics.showsDetailedCard` is true while *any* account's card is detailed; `DetailCardLayout.height` then adds the header's second line and the activity section, each at a named height so budget and drawing cannot drift. One frame serves every ring. Only the app's own `AppSettings` writes the metric (`drivesPanelMetrics`), so a test's instance cannot resize another test's frame.
- **An extension's metrics take no budget of their own** ([../extensions.md](../extensions.md#metrics)). A metric is a `ValueRow`; a trend line (`MetricTrendLine`, `chartHeight`) is about a limit row's height, so the card draws at most `DetailCardLayout.metricTrendLimit` (2) and reads at most four metrics, which fit `maximumHeight` beside a few limits and a balance. Lines are static; the extension's Settings pane has the hover readout.

## Dock, float, displays

`PanelPlacement`: `update(dock:)` changes placement **and** moves the window (`onChange` → `placePanel`). `record(...)` only stores; drag uses it because the window already moved. Settings must not use the store-only path (the rail is stranded). `PanelPlacement.layout(in:panel:rail:)` is the single source of truth for the window frame **and** the rail offset in it; drag and `placePanel` both use it.

- Drag anywhere; fuse to an edge within `dockDistance` **during** the drag, not on mouse-up. A free upright rail stays at least `dockDistance` from both sides while snapping is on (flush, it would look docked); a rail lying across, or any free rail with snapping off, goes flush to the sides (`PanelPlacement.sideMargin`). Held back, a rail lying across stopped 32pt short of a side while the hand kept moving.
- Off an edge the rail stands free **the way it already runs** (off the top it stays across, off a side upright); it turns only when it fuses to an edge of the other axis. An upright rail thrown at the bottom of the usable area (pointer within `dockDistance`) lies across, card opening up.
- **Snap to screen edges** (`AppSettings.snapsToEdges`, on by default; #85). Off, a drag never docks or turns the rail: it stands free the way it ran when picked up (`PanelPlacement.dragDock`), anywhere on the screen: its room is the whole screen rather than the usable area (`PanelPlacement.room`), so it can lie over the menu bar or beside the Dock, and while it reaches past the usable area it is raised to `.statusBar` (`PanelPlacement.isBeyond`, `applyLevel(for:beyond:)`) or it would be drawn under them. Switching snapping re-measures a free rail's ratios against the new room from where it is (`keepFreeRailInPlace`), so it does not jump by the Dock's or the menu bar's height. Choosing a position in Settings still docks it. Changing it re-places the panel (`onLayoutChange`); `PanelPlacement.snapsToEdges` is the controller's copy.
- **A card taller than its side of the screen scrolls** (`UsageDetailCard.maxHeight`, `PanelPlacement.cardRoom`). Lying across, the room (rail to the usable edge, less the gap) is computed at placement and every drag frame; a card that does not fit keeps that height and scrolls inside (bounce only when it scrolls), and the window keeps its size. Turning the card cannot help: it already opens to the roomier half, and mid-screen both are short (13" display: 498pt needed, 403 left). Down a side the card slides along the rail, unlimited. `CardRoomTests`.
- Any display, remembered by **UUID**, not `CGDirectDisplayID`; missing display → main. `didChangeScreenParametersNotification` re-places (not during a drag).
- Clamp each frame to the screen under the **pointer**, not the window's screen, or a second monitor is unreachable.
- Store the **rail's** position, never the window's: the window is much wider, and the rail's side flips at screen mid.

**Docked to the bottom** (`PanelDock.edge(.bottom)`, Settings → Position: *Bottom*):

- The rail stands on the screen's **own** bottom (`layout(bottomEdge:)` = `screen.frame.minY`), level with the Dock: `visibleFrame` removes the Dock's height across the whole width, and the empty stretch beside the Dock is where this rail goes. It slides by `horizontalRatio`, clamped to the visible sides; the card opens upwards (`PanelEdge.bottom`).
- The berth is the top dock's drawing rotated (`DockBerthShape`, winding kept; `BottomDockTests`), sliver along the bottom edge. Level `.statusBar`, so it is not under the Dock where they meet. Pulse cannot know where the Dock's icons end (no public API) and does not steer round them.
- Dragging: the pointer within `dockDistance` of the screen's own bottom docks; just above the Dock, short of that, the rail lies free across; with the Dock hidden the lines meet and docking wins. A docked rail stays while its own bottom is within `dockDistance` of the screen's bottom (a ring is 32pt or more above it, so the pointer rule would lift it on the first sideways frame).

**Two free placements: upright and across** (`PanelDock.floating(PanelEdge.Axis)`, Settings → Position: *Free across* / *Free upright*), stored in `panel.floatingAxis`; absent means upright.

- Across is the top dock's shape placed by both ratios (`PanelPlacement.layoutLyingFree`). Its card hangs down in the top half of the screen and stands up in the bottom half (`PanelEdge.bottom` as a card direction; the window's bottom is the rail's bottom); hanging down everywhere cut it off against the Dock.
- Notch attachment and `.statusBar` level follow the **dock** (`.edge(.top)`, plus `.edge(.bottom)` for the level), never `edge == .top`, which a free rail in the top half also has.
- **Lying free, the rail has its own proportions** (`DockLayout.freeAcrossWithLabels`: horizontal, not docked, labels on); the top dock's (10pt above and below, 30pt between rings) looked like loose icons on a free pill. The top dock keeps its stack either way.
  - **Default, figures under** (`labelsStackedFree`): 20pt between items, 14pt above and below; 86pt thick against the top dock's 78 (time arc off; with it on, both 15pt thicker: 5 between ring and figure, 10 on the ring's side).
  - **Figures beside** (`AppSettings.freeAcrossFiguresBeside`, Settings → Rings and figures, *Figures beside the rings*; `labelsBeside`): ring + gap + label budget (36 + 6 + 38), 18pt apart; 64pt thick and much longer. The label keeps its budgeted width so the ring does not slide, and is **always right of the ring**, whatever *Figure above the ring* says (issue #73).
- Every caller that locates a ring (`ringStep`, `ringCentreAcross`, `firstRingAlong`) takes `docked`. The horizontal window is as wide as the longer rail (`maximumLength`) and as tall as **this dock's** rail (`Layout.size(for:…docked:)`), so the top dock's budget is unchanged. `RailGeometryTests` covers no figures, figures under and figures beside; `FreeLyingRailProportionTests` pins the proportions.

**The time-until-reset arc pushes figures off.** It is drawn 5pt outside the ring, so `DockLayout.ringToTextSpacing` is 11pt instead of 6 while `AppSettings.showsWindowClock` is on (`PanelMetrics.showsWindowClock`, set before the change is announced so the rail is re-measured). Lying across with figures stacked, the ring's other side gets 10pt more (`DockLayout.crossInsets`); otherwise, with figures above the rings at the top dock, the arc sat 3pt off the rail's edge under a notch's housing (issue #78). 10 was picked by eye on the bundled app. Side rails and rails with figures beside or off are wide enough and get nothing extra.

### A frame is not granted until the window is on screen

`placePanel` measures the rail offset against the frame the window was **granted**: the panel is taller than a laptop's usable area and `constrainFrameRect` pulls it down (`RailOffsetTests`). A window never ordered in is not constrained yet (`setFrame` stores the request, `frame` returns it unchanged), so `show()` places, orders front, and **places again**: the first call avoids appearing at the origin, the second measures what was granted. Without it (1800×1169 display: asked y=76, granted y=0) the rail sat 76pt too low until the next re-place. Verified on a real `FloatingPanelController` (`railTop` after `show()` against a re-place); no test, since it needs a real window on a real screen.

### Follow the active display

`AppSettings.followsActiveDisplay` (off by default) + `ActiveDisplayFollower` + `PanelPlacement.move(toDisplay:)`. Issue [#16](https://github.com/qunqin24/Pulse/issues/16).

- **Active = the display holding the pointer**, not the key window or frontmost app (which can be elsewhere, and would need Accessibility permission).
- **One panel, moved**: only `display` changes; both ratios carry across.
- **Sampled on a 0.25s timer**, like `PanelPointerWatcher` (a global mouse-moved monitor goes silent over this app's windows and when the pointer rests). Fires once per crossing; runs only while the panel is visible **and** the setting is on; bails at once on one display.
- **Refusal is reported.** `move(toDisplay:)` returns `false` while the panel is held (`isPressed` / `isDragging`, as for `railLengthChanged`), so the display is re-offered next tick. The *same* display returns `true` and does nothing.
- The move is **recorded** like a drag, so turning the setting off leaves the panel where it was last carried.
- `didChangeScreenParametersNotification` calls `forgetLastDisplay()`, so unplugging the panel's monitor is not read as nothing to do while the panel sits on a fallback screen.

## Axis, not a third special case

`PanelEdge.axis`: stacks, flare, card unfold, which stored ratio is pinned, sliver side. Both shapes are drawn **once, facing right**, then transformed (mirror left, quarter-turn top); rotation, not reflection, preserves `UsageBubbleShape`'s winding.

**Top dock on a non-notch display is the physical edge, above the menu bar** (`FloatingPanel.topEdge(of:)`, `applyLevel(for:)`): placement uses `frame.maxY` and level `.statusBar`; away from the top and bottom docks the level is `.floating`, except for a free rail reaching past the usable area (snapping off).

**On a notch display, top docking attaches to the physical housing.**

- `PanelScreen.notch` reads `safeAreaInsets.top` and the width of the gap between `auxiliaryTopLeftArea` / `auxiliaryTopRightArea`, in global coordinates, and **centres it on the display**, not on the gap: the two areas can differ (791 and 788pt on a 14-inch display at 1800pt), which put the rail 1.5pt right of the housing, a red sliver beside its right edge when the surface was painted red and photographed. No hardcoded model dimensions or private APIs. Missing housing geometry falls back to the ordinary top dock.
- The rail centres beneath the housing without rewriting the remembered horizontal ratio.
- **Collapsed, nothing is drawn**: no sliver, enlarged housing or invisible grab target. When a limit has crossed the warning threshold and dock alert colour is on, `NotchAlertShape` draws a 2pt status edge just below and inside the housing.
- **Opening needs a dwell**: a `PointerEntryReporter` over the housing arms it, and the rail opens only if the pointer is still there after `PanelIntent.notchDwell` (250ms); see [input.md](input.md#hover-open--close-the-card). Leaving uses the 320ms grace period. With auto-collapse off it stays expanded; dragging, a held press and the panel menu keep it open.
- `PanelPointerWatcher` samples the same window, which now reaches the screen top. No second window, global event monitor or permission.
- **`NotchBerthShape` is the housing continued downwards**: continuous rounded bottom corners, and at the screen top a concave fillet the size of the **housing's own** (`NotchBerthShape.filletRadius`, 6.5pt fixed, control points at 0.55; Apple publishes no figure. Notch apps use 6pt, e.g. boring.notch after DynamicNotchKit, which read a touch less round than the housing's own beside it; 8 and 10 read rounder; 6.5 was picked by eye on a 14-inch MacBook Pro). The rail's flare (`DockLayout.flareWidth` / `flareHeight`, 38 × 24pt) read as a second, larger curve. Without the fillet the corners are square and nothing else fails, so `NotchGeometryTests` pins it. The frame still leaves `flareWidth` either side.
- **Nothing is added under the rail to answer the housing**: the black above the rings is the bezel, and matching its 38pt would put that much black under a 36pt ring. The surface is exactly the housing plus the rail with its usual symmetric padding; rings start right below the housing, the card after the body. Expanded width is the larger of the **free** rail's length and the housing.
- **Hit area** (`PanelHitArea.notchSurface`) is that body: it takes the docked ends' flare room back off (`flareHeight` each end, from `DockLayout.endPadding(docked:)`), else the notch rail was wider than the same rings standing free. The fillets sweep `flareWidth` further out; the drawing frame allows for them, the grab area does not. The pointer test is the drawn shape, not the rail rectangle.
- **Window budget** includes the housing height, open or closed, and the surface's minimum width **plus a fillet either side** (`NotchBerthShape.filletRadius`). With few rings the housing is the widest thing in the window, and at the small size wider than the card (205pt against 221pt on a 14-inch display at 1800pt), so budgeting the housing alone cut the fillets off square at the window's edges. `NotchGeometryTests.windowHoldsFillets` sweeps every size and ring count. Hover and card opening never resize it; display geometry changes may, and they and dragging refresh the notch rectangle through the rail's placement paths.

**Landing during a drag** ([../decisions/panel-frame.md](../decisions/panel-frame.md)):

- Docking to the top or bottom, and lying across at the bottom, is tested against the **pointer**, not the rail (a vertical rail is almost display-tall). The sides are too for a rail lying across: it is most of the screen wide, so its end reached a side long before the pointer neared the bottom, and a rail carried into a bottom corner stood up against the side (#85). An upright rail still docks by its own edge. `DragDockTests`.
- After an axis change, measure in the **landing** orientation and drop the grab offset (centre under the pointer).
- Do **not** infer a turn from rail size: floating drops end padding, so `landingRail != rail` off an edge re-centred and snapped. On that size change re-measure the grab to the rail **centre** so rings do not move.
- The landing **axis** comes from the dock being landed on, never `placement.edge` (a frame behind during a drag); its side, which only decides where the card opens, from the placement after `record` stores the new ratios.

## Liquid docking (experimental)

Behind **Settings → Experimental → Liquid docking** (`AppSettings.liquidDocking`, off by default; copied to `PanelPlacement.liquidDocking` like `snapsToEdges`). Off, a drag places the rail under the pointer on every event, exactly as before. On (studied from [codenotch](https://github.com/vinzdg/codenotch), MIT, which the user had used; approaches and some figures taken, no code):

- **The rail follows the hand on a spring** (`FloatingPanel.step`, driven by the screen's `CADisplayLink`, not a timer). A drag event only moves the goal; each display frame integrates a spring toward it (0.2s response, 0.84 damping carried; 0.3 / 0.74 settling after mouse-up, until within 0.3pt and 6pt/s) and places the window for where the rail is drawn. Joining an edge, letting go and settling back are the same spring aiming elsewhere.
- **A docked rail is pulled off, not dropped off.** It keeps its edge until the pointer has carried it `DropletTether.breakDistance` (80pt) away; another edge still wins at once. Meanwhile it is drawn `displayPull` in from the edge — 15% of the hand's speed at first (`grip`), meeting the pointer at the hand's speed at the break, so letting go is neither a jump in place nor in speed (the first version, `d²/break`, arrived at twice the hand's speed).
- **The gap is a neck** (`DropletTether.bridge`, unioned with the body): first the flare simply slides with the edge; then the contact narrows to a broad foot on the edge (at most 0.4 of a short rail's half length, or it read as a slab) and a waist that holds its width, then gives (`1 − u^2.2`). It thins with the pointer's pull (`PanelPlacement.tension`) as well as with how far the trailing rail has got, so a quick pull does not part a thick neck in one frame. At a notch the foot is the housing with its own 6.5pt fillets and the waist is kept below the housing (`mergesFoot` off).
- **Letting go keeps the docked window for `partingDuration` (0.22s)**: the rail already follows the hand inside it while the parted neck's two pieces draw back — into the body and into the edge (at a notch, into the housing). Only then does it stand free (`standFree`), the corners rounding into the capsule (`fusion`, ~0.14s); cut short if the rail would leave the window. A rail let go of beside the Dock glides above it on the spring instead of jumping.
- **Drawn onto an edge**, the rail starts where it was, on a thin neck (`attachThinning`) that thickens as the spring carries it in.
- **A quick throw stretches the surface**, up to 14% at 2600pt/s, front staying with the hand, 35% of that thinner across; docked, only along the edge (across it, the neck was pushed off the edge). The rings are not stretched.
- **Flares are fluid turns** (`DropletTether.fluidTurn`): heading a mix of a straight ramp and half a cosine, half each (`flareRamp`), so curvature eases in and out at both ends; the neck's own turns are round (ramp 0) — fully eased, a long thin neck read as a stick with corners. The docked berth's flares use it whether or not this is on.
- The follow records the placement in memory on each frame and **writes it once, when it stops** (`record(persist:)`). A press while a rail is still springing back is measured against the rail as drawn, `pull` in from its edge; the rail's size is re-read each frame, so a ring added mid-settle is placed at once.
- Everything pulled is drawn **outside the frame**, into the panel; the light panel's hairline mask reaches past the frame on every side but the screen's for that reason.
- Not verified: Liquid Glass on the pulled part.

## Flare, sliver, labels, scale

- `DockLayout.endPadding(docked:)`: floating drops the flare's padding so visible breathing room matches docked. Anything measuring the rail (ring centres, `PanelHitArea.slot`) must be told docked or not.
- Auto-collapse to a 6pt sliver (`AppSettings.autoCollapse`, default on) **only while docked** (at a notch the collapsed surface is empty instead). The sliver **is** `DockBerthShape` at `openness` 0 (`animatableData`), not a second view. Layout stays at full rail size so the card's geometry holds. Rings keep tracking areas only while expanded (`isInteractive`).
- The sliver takes usage colour past the warning threshold; at a notch, `NotchAlertShape` carries it.
- `sideRailShowsPercentages` default on; `topRailShowsPercentages` default off. Width stays `DockLayout.width` either way. Both flags live on `PanelMetrics` and in `.id(...)`.
- Left dock mirrors the panel. Floating, the silhouette is a capsule with **circular** ends (squircle ends become a lozenge at this width). Asymmetric chrome must handle both edges **and** both dock states.
- **`AppSettings.usesRoundEnds` (default off) switches the rail's ends and the card's tail together**; split, a round end could sit on the softened 46pt padding with the first ring hard against the curve.
  - **Off** (shipped): 26pt fourth-order superellipse corners (`DockBerthShape.appendCorner`), 24 × 38 flare, `verticalPadding` 46, `endRingOffset` 0, angled tail.
  - **On: a ring's outer edge (20pt at standard) sets every curve.** Rail ends are half-circles (`DockLayout.cornerRadius`, 32pt = 20 + the 12pt band beside the ring) on the end ring's centre line; the flare is that circle inside out (`flareHeight` = `flareWidth` = `cornerRadius`), meeting the corner as one S-curve. The end ring sits `endRingOffset` (8pt) inside the end's centre: 20pt of black over it, 12 beside (0, 8 and 12 compared; concentric read cramped). `verticalPadding` is derived (54pt); floating end padding is 22pt.
  - The card's corners are that 20pt radius in either style (`DetailCardLayout.cornerRadius`).
  - Round ends add 16pt to the rail, so the setting goes through `onLayoutChange?()` (re-place, fetch nothing) and rides on `PanelMetrics`: the AppKit frame comes from `DockLayout` before SwiftUI lays out.
  - Either way `cornerRadius + flareWidth` equals `width`, and `verticalPadding` clears `flareHeight` by 22pt.
- `labelAboveRing` (default off) keeps rail size but moves the ring inside the item; `DockLayout.ringOffsetInItem(on:)` is the one number drawing and hit testing share.
- `PanelSize` small/standard/large → `PanelMetrics.scale`, which every dock and card measurement reads.
- `showsSecondRing` (default off) changes nothing outside the ring's circle; it moves the activity mark and shrinks the icon disc, both budgets. [rings-and-surface.md](rings-and-surface.md)
