# Token spend

Owns: the Settings pane that answers *across everything I use, where did the work go* — its agents, its readers, and what may and may not be said about the figures. The per-provider history card is a different thing and lives in [refresh-and-data.md](refresh-and-data.md).

Source: [`SpendAgent`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/SpendAgent.swift), [`AgentLedgers`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/AgentLedgers.swift), [`AgentCache`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/AgentCache.swift), [`AgentUsageRecord`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/AgentUsageRecord.swift), [`AgentLogIO`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/AgentLogIO.swift), [`AgentSQLite`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/AgentSQLite.swift), the reader families under [`Usage/Readers`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/Readers), [`SpendSummary`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/SpendSummary.swift), [`ModelSpendSummary`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/ModelSpendSummary.swift), [`Recap`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/Recap.swift), [`TokenActivity`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/TokenActivity.swift), [`TokenActivityView`](../Sources/Pulse/Settings/TokenActivityView.swift), [`TokenSpendView`](../Sources/Pulse/Settings/TokenSpendView.swift), [`ModelSpendDetailView`](../Sources/Pulse/Settings/ModelSpendDetailView.swift). Every store, its default macOS path, its counters and its evidence: [token-spend-sources.md](token-spend-sources.md).

The span defaults to the last **week** and the reader's pick is kept across visits and launches (`AppSettings.spendSpan`; `SpendSpan.default` is `.week`). A stored value the picker no longer offers falls back to the week. The menu has three groups: rolling spans ending today (today, the last 7, 30 and 90 days), **calendar periods** (this month, last month — a closed month, stopping before this one — and this year; "last month" as a bill reads it is not the last 30 days), and everything or **Custom range**. A custom range is the reader's own first and last day, both counted (`AppSettings.spendCustomDays`, the last seven days until changed, kept like the span); a row of two date fields appears under the menu while it is chosen, neither after today and the first never after the last. Every span is a pair of local midnights, `SpendSpan.bounds`, either edge open, fed to `SpendSummary.of(_:starting:before:)` and `ModelSpendSummary.of(_:named:from:until:)` through `SpendSummaryCache.Window`; a span of one calendar day (today, or a custom range of one day) is drawn by the hour, as today always was. `SpendSpanBoundsTests`.

The pane is Settings-only and nothing on it reaches the rail: it is read off hundreds of megabytes of local stores rather than a live endpoint, and answers a sit-down question rather than a glance.

## Opt-in reading and cancellation

**Reading is off by default, including on upgrade.** `AppSettings.readsTokenSpend` has its own persisted key and does not call the quota providers' `onChange` hook. While off, the pane shows only its switch and an explanation, and opening it discovers no sources, reads no records/exports/caches and requests no prices. Claude Code's and Codex's account-history cards do not depend on the switch. The **detailed hover card**'s On this Mac section (Claude Code, Codex and every agent that borrows a provider's mark, through `AgentLedgers.ledger(for:)`) and the menu bar's account tabs do: they read only with it on ([ui/panel-geometry.md](ui/panel-geometry.md#detailed-card)).

Once enabled, the first visit checks the disk caches and reads changed sources. A completed snapshot, including an empty result and its read-limit notes, stays with the Settings window: returning from another sidebar pane shows it at once, with no discovery, fingerprinting, cache loading or price request. **Rescan** explicitly updates the figures and bypasses the complete-agent caches; transcript readers still reuse unchanged per-file entries. Progress names the source being read and its position in the detected list, not an estimated percentage.

- **One SwiftUI task** owns entry and manual rescans. Turning the switch off, navigating away or closing Settings cancels an unfinished one. `SpendReadState` keeps only completed snapshots across sidebar changes; an unfinished first read is retried on return.
- **Window visibility and the enabled flag are both in the task id**, even on other panes, because closing an AppKit window that retains its hosting view does not necessarily remove the SwiftUI view. Closing the window after navigating away therefore still releases the snapshot and every derived summary.
- **A read id** stops a cancelled predecessor from publishing results or clearing its replacement's loading state.
- **At most one completed snapshot per window.** Turning reading off or closing Settings releases the window's result; a rescan releases the old detail before allocating its replacement. Apart from the window, `AgentLedgers` keeps only the last finished scan for the background reader ([Caching](#caching)), dropped when reading is turned off.

**Cancellation is cooperative inside the readers.** Line and chunk reads, directory enumeration, record aggregation, SQLite row iteration and VM progress, and zstd output chunks check the caller's task; a synchronous whole-JSON/cache codec call finishes before the next checkpoint, and the reader does not carry on through the remaining sources. It is not a global flag, so cancelling this pane does not cancel an account-history task. A cancelled read is neither published nor saved as a complete cache, and cache writers recheck cancellation before writing; already completed valid caches remain usable.

## Pricing a model its own maker does not publish

`ModelPrices.providers` is twelve first-party vendors and stays that way: a reseller's markup is not the model's price. Some models are sold **only** through a plan (`deepseek-v4.1-flash` is sold by OpenCode Go and absent from DeepSeek's own models.dev entry), so the choice is the rate the tokens were bought at or no figure.

`SpendAgent.priceVendor` names the models.dev vendor an agent's plan is sold by (`opencode-go`, `kilo`, `cline-pass`). Those rates are stored **namespaced** as `vendor|id`, so only a lookup that asks for that vendor can reach them and they cannot collide with a first-party id. `ModelPrices.price(for:in:vendor:)` tries first-party spellings and aliases first; a first-party price always wins. An agent that calls model vendors directly has no `priceVendor`.

**One vendor is a supplement, not a models.dev vendor.** Devin prices its own `swe-2` and models.dev carries no Cognition entry at all, so the fetched table can never price it. `ModelPrices.supplement` holds Devin's published list rate ($3.00/1M input, $15.00/1M output, $0.30/1M cache input — https://docs.devin.ai/desktop/models) keyed `devin|swe-2`, merged into every table `prices()` returns — fetched, cached or empty — and never written to `model-prices-5.json`. A first-party entry still wins a shared id, so the merge only fills a gap. `.devinCLI` and `.devinDesktop` name `devin` as their `priceVendor`. The omp → devin card slice needs no vendor: every omp record routed to `devin` already carries the dollar figure omp billed (`usage.cost`), and recorded cost outranks every table — it is the price actually charged, not an estimate of one.

The vendor is passed through the shared pricing pass for **days, slots, model amounts and sessions**, so a priced session cannot coexist with an unpriced headline for the same tokens (`VendorPriceTests`). OpenCode and Kilo CLI share a database reader; each caller supplies its own vendor.

**Bulk pricing** uses a read-local `ModelPriceLookup`: one lazily built case-folded index over an immutable table, memoizing resolved rates **and misses** by model and vendor. Exact first-party IDs go straight to the original dictionary, so a read containing only those needs no index. It shares the single-lookup alias/precedence rules. A replacement price table gets a new lookup, so a stale miss cannot survive a price refresh; there is no process-global resolution cache.

## Token activity

Under the headline total sits a **Token activity** section (「Token 活动」): the last twelve months to today, one number per calendar day, drawn three ways behind a segmented control — **Daily** (a 53-week by 7-day grid of rounded squares), **Weekly** (one bar per week) and **Cumulative** (the running total from the first record). It appears in the combined view and, narrowed to that agent, under one agent; a model's detail has none. The choice is kept (`AppSettings.spendActivityView`, key `settings.spendActivityView`, default Daily; an unknown stored value falls back to Daily).

**It does not follow the span picker.** `TokenActivity.of(_:now:calendar:)` adds up `LedgerDay.tokens` over the ledgers the pane already holds (the same `Origin.supportsTokenSpend` gate as every total), reading no store. `SpendSummaryCache` computes it once per snapshot, day, calendar and agent (`ActivityKey`); a span change leaves it alone. Every ledger carries daily totals, and a source with only coarse timing still lands on its real day.

**The window** is `today − 1 year + 1 day`, pulled forward when it would need a 54th column, in whole week columns starting on `Calendar.firstWeekday` (Monday or Sunday follows the system). The first and last columns are partial; the total is exactly the drawn days.

**Nothing is invented.** A day before the first record across the counted agents (found over the whole history, not only the window) or after today carries no number: no bar, no point, and an all-empty column is not a bar. In the Daily grid, a day in the window before the first record is a **placeholder square** at half a quiet day's strength (`Week.unrecorded`), so a short history still fills the row and its month labels without reading as a quiet zero; a day after today has no cell. From the first record on, a day with no tokens is **quiet**, a real zero (a light neutral cell, a hairline in a week with data). With no record in the year the section is not drawn. A ledger that may be missing counts and contributed to the window adds "Counts may be incomplete." under the chart.

**Colour** is the pane's accent at five strengths: quiet, then four by the **quartiles of the days with work** (`TokenActivity.cuts`, `step(for:)`), so one enormous day (common with cache reads) does not flatten the year. A single active day is step 1. The accent is drawn as opacity over the surface so it reads in light and dark.

**Layout.** All three views share one horizontal scale of 53 week columns, so the month labels are shared and switching views moves nothing sideways.

- Month labels use the locale's abbreviation ("11月", "Nov") at the position of each month's 1st (column plus weekday row / 7), so months are 4 to 4.4 columns apart as the calendar has them; snapping to a month's first column gave an uneven 4 or 5. Labels run through the current month. When two would sit within three columns the earlier is dropped, so the newest month is always named; the last may run up to 12pt into the card's padding before being pulled back, so a narrow pane keeps the month before it; labels that still touch (by measured width) drop the earlier one.
- The columns always fill the row: cells shrink and grow with the pane (about 6pt at the settings window's 720pt minimum). A 13pt cap once left an empty strip that read as a half-drawn chart.
- The plot is `ColumnsLayout`, a `Layout` whose height is seven columns; an aspect ratio on a flexible view sized the grid from its own ideal instead of the offered width.

**Reading it.** Pointing at a cell, bar or point shows `ChartTooltipCard` (shared with `ChartHoverOverlay`): the date with year (a week as a clipped range; "Total through <date>" in the cumulative view) over an abbreviated token count. The grid hit-tests itself (`DailyGrid`); bars and the line use `ChartHoverOverlay`. VoiceOver gets one element for the chart, valued with the exact total and the active-day count, not 371 elements.

`TokenActivityRenderTests` draws all three views, light and dark, Simplified Chinese and English, at 460 and 900pt from synthetic data: `PULSE_ACTIVITY_PREVIEW=/tmp/activity swift test --filter TokenActivityRenderTests`.

## Model details

Every model row opens its token details, including when only one model is listed, and models beyond the initial eight remain reachable. Opening a model from the overview counts every agent that used it; opening one inside an agent keeps that agent as the scope; Back returns to the model list in that scope. The selected model is temporary navigation state; the span remains the saved preference.

The detail shows the model's token total and API cost estimate, the new-input/cache-hit/output split with amounts, daily and hourly token usage, contributions by agent with amounts, and a paged daily table with a sortable cost column. A model with nothing in a newly selected span stays open with an empty state. Opening a model and changing its span only summarize the ledgers in memory; **Rescan** is the explicit reread.

- **Figures.** Abbreviated figures expose exact counts through system help tags. A known zero is `0`; an unavailable daily breakdown or amount is `—`. Sorting a numeric column leaves unavailable values last in either direction and breaks ties by date.
- **Charts.** Daily and hourly charts use the same immediate `ChartHoverOverlay` as account history: anywhere in the plot selects the nearest column, including short and zero bars, with a guide and a compact label of date (with year) or hour plus `TokenCount.short` (万/亿 in Simplified Chinese, the corresponding units in the other East Asian languages, K/M/B in English). The label stays inside the chart at either edge without resizing the card or waiting for a help tag, and clears on leaving the chart, changing the data/span or leaving the view. Bars expose date/hour and exact count to VoiceOver; unavailable hourly detail stays unavailable rather than getting an invented series.

`ModelSpendSummary` matches the list's grouping key (`ledger.modelNames[rawID] ?? rawID`), including several raw IDs with the same published name. It adds only local and imported counts within the selected calendar span, fills quiet days, and keeps unpriced models' tokens. Per-model tallies and quarter-hour counts come from the shared reader's original buckets. A missing or incomplete split is unavailable, never an agent-wide split borrowed for the model; hourly counts must reconcile separately per agent, calendar day and raw model ID. The daily, agent and headline totals describe the same model and span.

### Model amounts

The shared pricing pass prices each raw model ID's own input, output, cache-write and cache-read counts at that model's published per-million rates; a missing cache rate falls back to the input rate.

- **Long-context tiers** (`cost.tiers` of type `context`, or `context_over_200k` alone; `272001` read as over 272,000) apply only where one record is one request — Claude Code's replies and Codex's readings — which carry the request's context (input, cache read and cache write together) as `TokenTally.contextBands`, banded at 128K, 200K, 256K and 272K. A request over a tier's threshold is billed at the tier in full, as OpenAI states its 272K tier; a tier between two bands applies from the next band up, never early. Anthropic's listings carry no tier; OpenAI's gpt-5.4, 5.5 and 5.6 do. Stores that add requests up are priced at base rates. (Historical, one Mac: 39 Codex requests passed 272K, about $5 more.)
- **One-hour cache writes** (`usage.cache_creation.ephemeral_1h_input_tokens`, kept as `TokenTally.cacheWrite1h` inside `cacheWrite`) are priced at twice the input rate, as Anthropic bills them; models.dev lists only the five-minute rate, which the other writes keep. (Historical, one Mac: 79% of Claude Code's writes were one-hour, and the five-minute rate hid about half the cache-write money.)
- `TokenCost` keeps the four amounts and `LedgerDay.modelCosts` retains them by raw model ID. Aliases and agents are combined **after** pricing, so two IDs with one display name keep their own rates. The model detail sums these saved amounts for its span and scope; it requests no price table on click and never allocates a day's combined cost by token share.

These are USD API estimates, not subscription charges. `costBreakdown`/`cost` are absent when none of a model's tokens can be priced; a published zero rate is a real zero. When only part can be priced, the amount is that part's subtotal and `unpricedTokens` names the excluded count; the headline labels a partial estimate, affected agent/day amounts are visibly marked, and category amounts cover the same priced subset. Missing per-model cost metadata or inconsistent token detail is left unpriced, not reconstructed from a combined total.

Positive amounts below one cent display as less than $0.01; true zero is $0.00; hover values use higher precision. Formatting follows the selected locale; the amounts are converted into the currency chosen in Settings → General, or stay in dollars while there is no rate ([refresh-and-data.md](refresh-and-data.md#currency)).

## An agent is not a provider

A **provider** is something Pulse draws a ring for: it reports a quota, has a settings pane, can be signed in to. An **agent** is something that has *spent* tokens on this Mac. The lists overlap but differ: Kimi's CLI, Kilo CLI and OpenCode leave transcripts and sell no plan Pulse watches, and most added sources (editor logs, per-product stores, export caches) are not providers at all. Folding them into `Provider` would put rings on the rail for products with no checkable limit, so the pane has its own type and nothing it does reaches the panel.

## Which agents, and on what evidence

The catalogue recognizes **54 sources**. Seven — Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Kilo CLI, Grok Build, Kimi CLI and Devin CLI — were read on a real machine with real work and keep their existing readers. The other 47 use reader families checked only against original synthetic stores; their observations, prerequisites and limits are in [token-spend-sources.md](token-spend-sources.md).

**That distinction is the admission rule.** A second-hand format spec pins a shape against change; it does not prove the shape, and a wrong guess fails silently as a wrong number. Every added reader therefore follows the same discipline: a store that reports no tokens contributes no record (never a zero), an estimate is not shown as usage, and a source that needs an export says so.

The pane never implies all 54 yield tokens on a fresh install. Most need their product installed and used, and the six export/cache clients (`cursor`, `antigravity`, `trae`, `warp`, `hindsight`, `mcode`) need a prior export or capture, or a file in Pulse's `UsageImports/<client>` folder.

Sources detected but yielding no counted tokens appear under **No usage data read** (「未读取到用量」), judged over the full scanned history rather than the span; it describes what Pulse read, not proof the source was never used.

### How the work is read

- `SpendAgent` is the whole catalogue (54 cases). `sourceID` is the canonical string the readers dispatch on; `hasCapturedValidation` marks the seven checked against a real store; `reportsTokenCounts`/`requiresUsageExport` say whether a token figure is a measurement and whether the store is an export rather than a native log.
- `AgentLedgers.scan` returns one `UsageLedger` per present agent plus that scan's read-limit notes. Claude Code and Codex are **delegated** to `UsageLedgerReader`, the per-provider card's reader. The other five legacy agents keep their own store readers (`OpenCodeStore`, `GrokStore`, `KimiCLIStore`, `DevinCLIStore`). `KimiCLIStore` reads the Kimi CLI's `~/.kimi/sessions` and its successor Kimi Code's `$KIMI_CODE_HOME/sessions` (default `~/.kimi-code/sessions`), both under the Kimi Code provider. The legacy log names no model and stays unpriced as `kimi (unnamed)`. Kimi Code writes each request three times (a `usage.record` line, the `step.end` event and the assistant message's `meta.usage`); only `usage.record` is counted, because it names the model, with the user's endpoint prefix dropped (`kimi-code/k3-256k` → `k3-256k`). `kimi-for-coding` is the plan's moving alias and stays unpriced. A request found in both stores, matched by its second and its four counts, is counted once from Kimi Code's copy. Everything added since goes through `AgentRecordReaders`, one router over six families in [`Usage/Readers`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/Readers): session logs (Group A), editor logs (B), databases (C), per-product stores (D), exports/captures (E) and Copilot (F).
- `AgentRecordReaders.inputs(client:home:environment:)` names every file a client would be read from — metadata registries and referenced databases included, and roots that do not exist yet — so the cache watches the real sources. `read(client:roots:)` returns `AgentUsageRecord` **increments** and read-limit notes together (DSH and Zed collect both in one decode pass, avoiding a second decompression); separate `records` and `notes` entry points remain. Readers read; they never price.
- Devin's Desktop route also receives the native databases. A capture matched unambiguously to a session with usable database counters is excluded before pricing; metadata alone never suppresses a capture. See [token-spend-sources.md](token-spend-sources.md#cross-source-routing-and-dedup).
- `AgentUsageLedger.build` turns records into the same days, models, sessions and quarter-hour slots the transcript readers make, through the shared `UsageLedgerReader.slotKey`/`.price`. `AgentLogIO` and `AgentSQLite` are the shared file/JSON/time and read-only-database helpers, so no two readers disagree about "malformed" or "missing".
- **A read a reader cannot finish is stated, not silently short.** History it cannot decode (no local zstd library, a corrupt frame, a payload over the size bound) is reported through `AgentRecordReaders.notes`, the pane says some compressed records could not be read, and that read is **not** persisted ([Caching](#caching)). User cancellation discards the result instead; it is not a diagnostic.

### Unclassified, incomplete and coarse are three different things

All three can be present at once, and each is surfaced on its own.

- **Unclassified.** A record's tokens are the four classified kinds **plus** `unclassifiedTokens`: work reported as a bare or session total without a kind. They count in the day, model, session and unpriced figures and are listed per raw model in `LedgerDay.modelUnclassifiedTokens`, but are **never** put into `input` and **never** costed. Views keep their known categories and add an **Unclassified** row (and day-table column) when a remainder is present; a bare total shows input, cache and output as **Not reported**, not measured zeroes. Known kinds plus the remainder must reconcile with each day's total (and each raw model's in the model detail); broken or missing metadata makes the split unavailable. No remainder is ever derived by subtracting kinds from a total.
- **Incomplete (`isPartial`).** The source read its counts but cannot prove they are the whole of its work — an ambiguous cache or reasoning relation, a lane that may not cover the store's lifetime, a row with no identity another lane might also hold. It is a **known subset**. `AgentUsageLedger.build` propagates it to `UsageLedger.hasPartialCounts`, then `SpendSummary` and `ModelSpendSummary`, and the pane says **"Counts may be incomplete."** It adds, guesses and reprices nothing.
- **Coarse (`isAggregate`).** The record's only timing is session- or report-level. It lands on its real calendar day but in no quarter-hour bucket, and a ledger with `hasAggregateTiming` withholds the hour profile for the whole scope. Its session still keeps **calendar-day buckets** (event and aggregate records alike in a mixed session), so Today does not include yesterday's report. `SpendSummary` uses those day buckets when a session has no trustworthy quarter-hours; only older in-memory ledgers with neither kind use the whole-session fallback.

`UsageLedger.Origin` says where figures came from: `.localTranscripts` (a built-in reader of a native log), `.importedRecords` (a capture, a dropped log or another tool's store; the six export/capture clients), `.providerStatistics` (a provider's account-wide reply) and `.providerLogs` (a provider's own request log with its charged cost, e.g. OpenCode Go's console). Only the first two can be priced and are drawn here (`Origin.supportsTokenSpend`); the account-wide two are **excluded from every total**, so no total mixes in a figure half of which cannot carry a cost.

## What the readers agree on

- **Money is the recorded cost where the store writes one, `ModelPrices` where it does not.** omp's `usage.cost` prices each message at the amount the agent itself billed; the same rule would serve any record carrying `recordedCost`. A store's own cost **column** or a provider-reported cost (OpenCode, Cursor, Warp, Unsloth, Gajae Code, Junie, Fx and others) is still **not** carried into priced totals: it reflects what that app believed at the time, is zero for plans it has no rate for, and would mix two differently sourced figures. Records without a recorded cost price from the table, and a model with no recorded cost and no published price is counted and not costed.
- **A store with no counters contributes no record.** Crush and Warp report cost or request counts and no tokens; Freebuff, Kiro's estimate-only variants and legacy Command Code/ZCode transcripts without a `usage` block leave only character estimates. None becomes a token number or a zero.
- **A model with no published price is counted and not costed**, after its spelling is resolved ([below](#one-model-several-spellings)).
- **`ModelPrices` covers twelve vendors, not two.** With only Anthropic and OpenAI, four of the seven original agents read $0.00. IDs are unique within a vendor but not across all 213 models.dev vendors, so the list is deliberate and ordered; `github-copilot` is left out because it re-lists other vendors' models.
- **Reasoning tokens count as output**, as every price list bills them.
- **A copied record is counted once.**
  - Claude Code replies are kept by message id; `scan` counts each in the file it first appeared in.
  - A Codex fork's readings are kept by the running total they reached; any other Codex session adds its readings up and keeps only the totals, for forks to be checked against. Non-fork files are ordered ahead of forks.
  - A Codex Desktop sub-agent (`session_meta.forked_from_id`) opens with its parent's running total and a `rollout-N` turn replaying the parent's readings: its first reading counts only its own request (`total − last_token_usage`) and the replayed turn counts nothing.
  - Older forks (April 2026) replay in ordinary UUID turns, dropped only because the parent's file counted the same running total first; a fork whose parent was deleted before Pulse read it still counts that history (a parent Pulse did read keeps holding the replay back through its archived totals, [History the tools have deleted](#history-the-tools-have-deleted)). A non-fork reading is never dropped this way, since two unrelated sessions can reach the same small total. A fork's timings are left out, because its lines all carry the instant it was made.
  - Historical, one Mac: 21 forks had added 2.3 billion tokens of their parents' history. `~/.codex/archived_sessions` is read beside `sessions`.
  - **One copy of each rollout.** A session's own readings are added up, not claimed, so a rollout left in both `sessions` and `archived_sessions` (a backup put back, a copy made by hand) was counted twice ([#95](https://github.com/qunqin24/Pulse/issues/95)). Files are matched by name, which carries the session id, and only the longest copy is read: a rollout is only appended to. The same holds for copies gone at once and for an archive that kept both before this; the archive file itself is not rewritten. Per-file cache entries were already right, so `ledger-9` was not bumped.
- **Quarter-hour buckets and one pricing function.** `UsageLedgerReader.slotKey` and `.price` are shared, so an agent read from a database is bucketed and priced exactly as a transcript is.

## One model, several spellings

A model id that misses the table is not free; it is a bill that silently reads zero. `ModelPrices.price(for:in:)` resolves, in order: the id as written, the id case-folded, then a short list of **aliases, each a named product's known habit**:

| Written | Means | Whose habit |
|---|---|---|
| `grok-4.6-build` | `grok-4.6` | Grok Build tags its own build |
| `k3-256k`, `kimi-k3-256k` | `kimi-k3` | a context window is the same model with more room |
| `k3`, `k2p6` | `kimi-k3`, `kimi-k2.6` | Kimi's CLI abbreviates the version |
| `gpt-5-6-sol-medium` | `gpt-5.6-sol` | Devin writes dashes for dots and an effort on the end |
| `gemini-3.8-flash-n` / `-control` / `-xpe` | `gemini-3.8-flash` | Antigravity's variants of one Flash, one published rate; `-n` seen in a store, the others as reported (#90) |
| `gemini-3.5-flash-extra-low` | `gemini-3.5-flash` | Antigravity's lowest Flash effort; `-low` alone left `gemini-3.5-flash-extra` (#90) |
| `gemini-3-1-pro` | `gemini-3.1-pro-preview` | Devin's clients spell the version with dashes and Google lists the preview build |
| `minimax-m3` | `MiniMax-M3` | case only |

**Rules, not fuzzy matching.** A loose match prices a model at another model's rate, a wrong number that looks right; `grok-build-0.1` is a real xAI model and must not be stripped to `grok`. A lookup that still misses stays unpriced (historical: eleven ids on one Mac, four of them `…-free` models that genuinely cost nothing).

## Titles and projects come from the transcript

**Project identity is the full stated working directory.** `/work/client-a/api` and `/work/client-b/api` are separate projects; the same directory used by two agents is one. Repeated/trailing separators are normalized without resolving symlinks, changing case or consulting the filesystem.

- `<repo>/.claude/worktrees/<name>` (the worktree Claude Code gives a subagent) counts as `<repo>`, whether read fresh or from an older cache (`UsageProject.repository(of:)`).
- **A scratch folder a tool makes is no project.** Codex Desktop's `…/Documents/Codex/<yyyy-mm-dd>/<slug of the first prompt>` (a chat with no project) and DeepSeek Harness's `…/deepseek-harness/default-workspace` read as no project, like a transcript that states no directory. It is a pure path rule (`UsageProject.isScratchDirectory`; only a date-shaped component after `Documents/Codex` with a slug under it counts), applied when a project is made and again when `AgentCache` reads a ledger kept before the rule.
- **Display names** are the basename, with parent components added when names collide, resolved in basename groups once per summary (unrelated projects never scan each other); ambiguous directories use their shortest distinct suffix. Session rows use the same names as the project list.
- **Label-only sources.** Labels are scoped to their agent and never matched to a directory by name. Claude Code's fallback folder encodes separators as dashes and cannot be reversed reliably, so its full folder identity is kept apart from its guessed short label and is never merged with an explicit `cwd`.

**A session's title** is the one the user set (`custom-title`), else the words the conversation opened with, trimmed to a line. A command envelope (`<…>`) or the sandbox caveat is not a title. Claude Code's sidechains have no prompt and so no title; the row falls back to the project.

- **The user's title is looked for on every line.** Claude Code writes `customTitle` on rename, often long after `cwd` and the opening prompt; a parser that stopped once it had both lost every rename. The last valid custom title wins; an unreadable one (empty, or an envelope) leaves the title already found.
- **Codex writes more user-role messages than the user does.** `UsageLedgerReader.codexTitle(in:)` skips the opening `# AGENTS.md instructions for …`, `<environment_context>` and `<recommended_plugins>`; later `<turn_aborted>`, `<subagent_notification>` and `<image>` envelopes; and the opening of a review session. It unwraps the IDE extension's `# Context from my IDE setup:` wrapper to the text after `## My request for Codex:`. The title is the first message left, first part only (the rest of an injected message can be a transcript). `CodexPromptCache` names its rows the same way.
- **Links are cleaned out** (`UsageLedgerReader.title(from:)`, Claude Code and Codex). `[text](url)` reads as `text`, `[url]` and `<url>` as the url, and whitespace collapses. A message whose first line is only a link is named for the first line below that is not one; a message that is only a link reads as `host/first-path-segment/…` (no scheme, brackets, query or rest of path). The 70-character limit applies after cleaning.
- **A review is a kind of session, not an untitled one.** Codex runs its own session to assess another's planned action; it opens with `The following is the Codex agent history …`, has no words of the user's and, in a scratch folder, no project (historical: about 200 on one Mac). The reader marks it (`Scanned.isReview` → `FileCache.Entry.isReview` → `UsageLedger.Session.isReview`, kept through the cache and `SpendSummary`) and takes no title after it. The prompt-cache lists mark it the same way.
- **A file name is never a title.** Every list that names a session goes through `SessionLabel`: the title, else "Codex review" for a review, else the project, else "Untitled conversation". `UsageLedger.Session.name` (a uuid, a `rollout-…` name) is a stored identity, never drawn. A subtitle adds the project only when the label is not already the project (`SessionLabel.namesItself`). The Token spend session list, the card's prompt-cache line and Settings' prompt-cache list share it.

**A conversation can be several files.** Claude Code writes each subagent's transcript to `<project>/<session>/subagents/agent-<id>.jsonl` beside `<project>/<session>.jsonl`. Their tokens count in days, models, projects and totals like any file's; the session list folds them into the parent's row (`UsageLedgerReader.sessionFile(of:)`, a path rule): the parent's id, name, title and directory, with tokens, money and quarter-hour buckets summed and `start`/`end` the earliest and latest. Folding happens after `scan` has counted every reply once, so a reply copied into a subagent file is not counted twice, and a parent whose every reply was counted elsewhere still names the row. Subagents whose parent file is gone make one row, named for the project folder. The per-file cache is unchanged.

**A session is counted by the part of it inside the span.** Sessions carry their own priced quarter-hour buckets, and a project's total over a span is the sum of the buckets inside it — the span's own arithmetic, never the whole conversation or a ratio-based share. A session row carries the span's portion; its `start` and `end` stay the conversation's own. Unpriced counts travel with every session and its quarter-hour/calendar-day buckets, and the span sums them alongside tokens and money, including unclassified tokens from aggregate reports. Session and project amounts render like agent/day amounts: `—` when all tokens are unpriced, a subtotal marked `*` when only part is priced (explained in the footnote), `$0.00` for a published zero rate. A ledger from before sessions kept buckets falls back to counting a session whole when it ended inside the span — a coarse rule for old data only.

## The recap and `--recap`

`Recap` ([`Recap.swift`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/Recap.swift), built by [`RecapBuild.swift`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/RecapBuild.swift)) is one calendar day's, week's, month's or year's figures for the shareable recap cards: **facts only, no copy**, and a figure Pulse cannot stand behind is `nil`, never zero. `Recap.build(_:from:prices:now:calendar:)` builds it from the same `[SpendAgent: UsageLedger]` the pane holds; it reads nothing itself.

**Everything is added up by `SpendSummary`**, through `SpendSummary.of(_:from:until:)`, the calendar-bounded form of `of(_:overLast:)`: `until` is exclusive; days, quarter-hours and session buckets outside `[from, until)` are cut; the series is padded from `from`; an `until` not after `from` is no days; streaks stay whole-history. So the recap cannot count a span differently from the pane. A running period ends at the end of today (`isInProgress`), and the **previous period is cut to the same number of days** (October 1–5 against September 1–5), clamped to its length (March against February); `previousTokens` is nil when nothing was recorded then.

What `build` works out itself:

- **Hours** (`hours`, `peakHour`, `lateShare`, `persona`) come from the contributing stores that record a time of day. A store with only session- or report-level timing (`hasAggregateTiming`) is left out of them alone; its tokens are counted in `untimedTokens` and stated under the timetable and the poster (`RecapDeck.hoursNote`). Excluding the whole figure instead once let one tool with 0.1% of a month remove the timetable, rhythm card and persona. Nil only when no quarter-hour work was recorded. The peak is the earliest hour among equals. `lateShare` is the 21:00–04:59 share of quarter-hour tokens, kept for `--recap` only.
- **Busiest hours.** The cards do not show that fixed band, which read as a claim about the reader while saying the same hours to everyone. `Recap.busiestHours` is the four consecutive hours (wrapping past midnight, earliest start among equals) with the most tokens; the rhythm card's sentence, its lime clock bars and the timetable's figure all name that window.
- **Persona** is a plain majority (more than half; exactly half is not) of the hour tokens, checked in order: `nightOwl` 21:00–04:59, `earlyBird` 05:00–09:59, `dayShift` 10:00–17:59, else `allDay`.
- **Late nights** (`lateNights`, `latestMinute`) come from stretches of work (`Recap.workdays`): every quarter-hour with tokens and every session's last record, split wherever nothing happened for three hours (`workBreak`); a stretch over twenty hours (`longestWorkday`) is a process left running and is skipped. A stretch belongs to the day it began, or to the evening before when it began before 05:00 (`nightEndsAtHour`). `latestMinute` is the latest a stretch ended, in minutes after its day's midnight (past 1440 into the morning; shown on a clock). `lateNights` counts stretches that ran past their day's midnight; a fixed 00:00–04:59 band capped an all-nighter at 04:45 and gave someone who stopped before midnight no figure. Day-level-only work has no instants, so where `hours` is nil both are floors.
- **Cache hit rate** is `UsageLedger.cacheHitRate`'s rule (`UsageLedger.cacheReading(in:)`, shared) over the contributing agents that vouch for their counts. One whose counts may be missing, or that has tokens no kind can claim, is left out of the rate and its tokens are counted in `cacheUnmeasuredTokens`, which the cards state; taking the rate away instead once let one small tool remove the cache tile from every card. An agent whose store records no cache (`SpendAgent.reportsCacheReads`) is left out silently, not counted as misses. Nil when no measured cache input remains.
- **Cache savings** are, per model, the work with no cache at all — reads and writes both billed at the input rate (`TokenTally.withoutCache`) — less what it was billed at (`TokenTally.costBreakdown`, long-context tiers included), at the price `ModelPriceLookup` returns for that raw id and the agent's `priceVendor` (the lookup the ledgers were priced with), from each day's `LedgerDay.modelTallies`, nothing prorated. A model with no price, or a day without per-model detail, adds nothing, so the figure is a floor; nil when no priced cache read exists. A model with no stated cache rate saves nothing.
- **Money** is nil for a day, month or total where no work had a price (`UsageLedger.shownCost`) and for days and months with no work; a part-priced figure is its priced subtotal. A model's money is its raw ids' own `LedgerDay.modelCosts`, grouped by display name; nil where none was priced.
- **Streaks are the period's own** (`Recap.streaks`, over `days`): the longest run inside the period, and the run ending on its last day (today, or yesterday while today is quiet, for a running period).
- **`unpricedTokens`** is `SpendSummary.unpricedTokens`: above zero, `cost` is a floor and the cards say so ([recap.md](recap.md#nil-means-left-out)).
- **Calendar**: `Recap.calendar` (Gregorian, Monday-first), not `Calendar.current`; the recap keeps the one it was built with ([recap.md](recap.md#one-calendar)).
- Models, projects and agents are heaviest first, with shares **of the recap's tokens**. An agent's `activeDays` is `SpendSummary.activeDays` over that agent alone, and its `activeDates` (`days` in the `--recap` JSON, oldest first) are those days; the opener's per-agent strips are drawn from them.

### The recap window

The pane's first group holds a **Usage recaps** row (reading on only) with one **Open** button. It opens the recap window ([recap.md](recap.md#the-window)) on the month `RecapPeriods.defaultMonth` picks from the earliest record in the pane's ledgers: the month that just ended during the first seven days of a month, the running one after that. Day, week and year are the window's own switch; no menu carries an entry for it. The window reads through `RecapSource.load`, which reuses the scan `SpendWarmer` keeps rather than reading the stores again; with reading off it sends the person back to this pane and **does not switch reading on**.

### `Pulse --recap`

```bash
./build.noindex/Pulse.app/Contents/MacOS/Pulse --recap 2026-10-18        # a day
./build.noindex/Pulse.app/Contents/MacOS/Pulse --recap week-2026-10-12   # the seven days from that date
./build.noindex/Pulse.app/Contents/MacOS/Pulse --recap 2026-09           # a month
./build.noindex/Pulse.app/Contents/MacOS/Pulse --recap 2026              # a year
./build.noindex/Pulse.app/Contents/MacOS/Pulse --recap                   # this month
```

A week is taken as written: `week-` and the date its seven days start on, not the week holding that date, so the command does not depend on the Mac's first weekday. `period` in the JSON is the same key. A date not on the calendar is refused.

Prints the `Recap` as sorted, pretty JSON on stdout and exits ([`RecapReport.swift`](../Sources/Pulse/Usage/RecapReport.swift)): `period`, `start`/`end` (local dates; `end` is the first day after the span), `inProgress`, the totals (`unpricedTokens` beside `cost`), `days`, `months` (a year), `hours`, `latestMinute` (minutes after the midnight of the day the stretch began; above 1440 for the next morning), `persona`, `models`, `agents`, `projects`, `cacheHitRate`, `cacheSavings`, and the period's streaks. **A missing figure is absent**, not null or zero. Nothing is translated.

It reads as the pane does, through `RecapSource.load` (`AgentLedgers.scan()` over the agents present, the same price table), and **only while Token spend reading is on**: otherwise it says so on stderr and exits 2 (a bad argument also exits 2). It stores no setting and writes nothing beyond the readers' ledger and price caches. It is dispatched before `LegacyDefaults.migrateIfNeeded()`, like `--json` ([json-output.md](json-output.md)), and keeps the main queue running (`dispatchMain`) rather than parking the main thread, because the scan reports progress on the main actor.

## Caching

**Prices.** `ModelPrices` keeps the table with its original fetch time, in memory and in `model-prices-5.json` (plan-vendor prices and long-context tiers). It is valid for 24 hours; the next price read after that fetches a replacement even if Pulse stayed open, and concurrent readers share one download. A failed download keeps the previous table and timestamp (or returns no prices if none was saved) and may retry on a later read after five minutes; there is no background price timer. A version 4 table (no tiers) is an offline fallback only and never delays the upgrade download. **Rescan** follows the same freshness and retry rules; returning to a completed pane reads no prices.

**Ledgers** have two cache layers:

- `UsageLedgerReader` caches **per transcript file** (`ledger-9-*.bin`), keyed on size and modification date, and prices afresh each read, so a price change does not mean rescanning hundreds of megabytes. The raw-token cache is written only when a file was added, changed or removed (repricing an unchanged scan encodes and writes nothing); a removed file's entry leaves only after its counted share is in `archive-ledger-<provider>.json` ([below](#history-the-tools-have-deleted)). Deduplication computes each file's first slot and fork status once, then orders: non-fork files first, then oldest work, then file name (Codex names a rollout for when it was made), then path.
- **The cache is bytes, not JSON** (`TranscriptCacheCodec`, `ledger-9-*.bin`). Read whole on every history read, Claude Code's had grown to 13 MB of JSON (345 transcripts, 38k replies by id); `JSONDecoder` was ~92% of a card's history read and left ~92 MB behind, every reply's quarter-hour and model a string of its own. The bytes decode to the very `FileCache` the JSON did, nil kept apart from empty, so `scan` counts exactly as before; every string is written once in a table and shared after reading. On that cache (test build): decode 230 → 44 ms, write 217 → 76 ms, file 12.2 → 6.2 MB, held after reading 92 → 22 MB. Same number, new extension: the content is unchanged, and a new number would have thrown away the record of transcripts deleted since the last scan. The `.json` of the same number is read once (`FileCache.isFromJSON`, written out at the next scan even with nothing changed) and removed once the bytes are down. A file that does not read whole is no cache, as a JSON that failed to decode was. `TranscriptCacheCodecTests`.
- **Slot keys back to dates go through `SlotDates`** (`UsageLedgerReader.slotDate`), not `DateFormatter.date(from:)`: pricing a ledger and listing its sessions turn every quarter-hour key back into a date, and through the formatter that was ~14µs a key — measured in Instruments as most of `priced` and `sessions` on a card's history read. The digits are taken out and a `Calendar` in the formatter's own zone asked (~1.9µs); a key in another shape, one that does not read back as its own fields, or one within a day of a clock change (where an hour repeats and the two pick different instants) goes to the formatter. `SlotDatesTests` holds every quarter-hour of two years in six zones against the formatter.
- `AgentCache` caches **the whole ledger** per agent (`agent-13-<agent>.json`), since these stores are read from several roots with nothing finer to keep. A root's own size and date are **not** a valid key: appending to a log inside Grok's or Kimi's store directory does not move the directory, and a database's `-wal` may not yet be checkpointed into the `.db`. The stamp is a digest of the **real inputs across every root**: roots standardized, ordered and deduplicated, each contributing its own identity line (so two empty stores differ, and adding or removing a root moves the digest); a directory contributes every regular file beneath it recursively, hidden files and dot-directories included, by path, size and modification date; a database contributes the `.db`, `-wal` and `-journal`. The `-shm` is left out because merely opening the store touches it.
- **The price table is part of the `AgentCache` key**, because money is part of the kept ledger. The stamp carries a digest of `ModelPrices`' rates and names: an unchanged refetch invalidates nothing, one changed rate invalidates the ledger, and an **empty** table is its own stamp, so a cold load with prices rejects an offline `$0.00` cache.

**A new ledger is persisted only if** the reader reported no limit, the task was not cancelled, and the store's fingerprint still matches after the read. Otherwise the result stays with the open pane only, so a later build that can decode the same files is not blocked by a partial cache; there is no retry loop. Notes travel with the scan's snapshot rather than being rediscovered. A valid cache hit has no notes, because a cache is only written from a complete read.

**Background reading.** With the switch on, `SpendWarmer` reads twenty seconds after launch, the moment the switch is turned on, and every ten minutes, at utility priority, one read at a time. `AgentLedgers` keeps the last finished scan (`keptSnapshot`) — one snapshot, replaced, dropped when the switch goes off — so the pane opens on figures even after the window was closed. A kept scan older than two minutes (`SpendWarmer.paneFreshness`) is shown at once and reread behind the figures with no spinner or progress row; with none kept, the pane reads as above. (Historical, one Mac: a warm pass over fifteen agents' stores took about 0.1–0.2 s; the waits were stores changed since the last read.)

**Summaries.** Loading and summarizing are separate cancellable tasks. `SpendSummaryCache` aggregates on its own actor, keeping at most one overview, one agent and one model summary per window. Its base key is the completed snapshot's revision, span, calendar, current calendar day and selected language (project-name suffixes name agents). Opening a model reuses the overview and matching agent summary; a new snapshot or window invalidates all three; closing Settings or disabling reading releases the cache. A cancelled or superseded request cannot publish. Changing the span or returning to the pane recomputes the calendar-window totals from the snapshot without reading files, so a new day can change Today while the readings stay from the last scan.

- **A new span keeps the figures on screen** until its own land (`SpendSummaryCache.Request.canKeepShowing`); swapping the page for a spinner lost the scroll position and looked like a rescan.
- **A new scope waits.** Opening another agent or model shows a small progress indicator rather than the previous figures under a new heading. Returning from a model shows its already-computed parent at once, preserving the model-list scroll target; a quiet refresh of the same scope may keep its previous figures.

**Cache file numbers are part of the contract, including reader semantics.**

- `PulseStorage.removeSupersededFiles` deletes at launch every `agent-<n>-*` and `ledger-<n>-*` whose `n` is below `AgentCache.version` / `UsageLedgerReader.cacheVersion`, by rule, so a bump needs no name added to a list (the old list missed every `agent-` file). Only unnumbered and `model-prices-` names are listed.
- `archive-*` files are never superseded: nothing can rebuild them, so they are unnumbered and change shape by migrating ([below](#history-the-tools-have-deleted)).
- `ledger-9-*.bin` holds raw transcript buckets, each file's reply timings, and Claude Code's replies by message id (a resumed or forked conversation opens with a copy of the old history, same ids and times; `scan` counts each id once, for the file whose work came first — historically 28% of tokens on one Mac were such copies). Pricing changes need no ledger bump. Because an unchanged file is never read again, reader fixes do: an earlier bump from `ledger-4` stopped taking Claude Code's output from a reply's first line (a reply is written over several lines with a climbing count; about a quarter of output went uncounted); `ledger-8` stopped titling Codex sessions with injected context; `ledger-9` added `isReview` and link-unwrapped titles.
- `agent-13-*.json` requires an explicit version, each session's calendar-day buckets, the origin, aggregate/partial flags, model amounts, unclassified-token detail, and unpriced counts on sessions and their buckets. Earlier or unknown versions and missing required fields are rejected and rebuilt; there is no silent zero-default migration. Bumps: 13, Kimi's ledger also reads Kimi Code's store and names its models; 12, Antigravity's `gemini-3.8-flash-n`, `-control` and `-xpe` rows, unpriced until then, are priced as `gemini-3.8-flash` (#90); 11, Grok Build's `inputTokens` already holds `cachedReadTokens` and its `outputTokens` the `reasoningTokens` (their sum is its own `totalTokens`), so the reader subtracts the read and adds nothing, where it had counted both twice (#91); and Antigravity's `gemini-pro-default` rows, unpriced until then, are resolved by their label (#90); 10, reader fixes in Devin CLI, OpenCode, Codebuff, Copilot and Gemini; 9, OpenCode 2's tables (an unchanged store's version 8 cache held none of its requests); 8, unpriced counts on sessions and buckets; 7, full-directory project identity; 6, invalidating version 5's incorrect vendor pricing, duplicated Devin captures, whole aggregate-session windows and active-branch-only Command Code totals.

**Zstd** is a local system library resolved by explicit path, never bundled; a machine without one reports the store through a note instead of counting it as empty. Details: [token-spend-sources.md](token-spend-sources.md#compression-is-a-local-library-not-an-os-guarantee).

## History the tools have deleted

The transcripts are Pulse's only record, and the tools prune them: Claude Code deletes sessions older than `cleanupPeriodDays`, other agents clear their session folders, and users delete conversations. Without an archive, a deleted file's tokens, money and hours left the totals and recaps weeks later, and `Recap.recordsBegin` moved later (historical: eight Claude Code sessions from May to July were already gone on one Mac, 2026-10-09). Pulse keeps what it has read, in a file of its own per source beside the caches.

**Claude Code and Codex, file by file** (`TranscriptArchive`, `archive-ledger-<provider>.json`). When a cached transcript is gone from disk, the scan counts it once more beside the live files, so the reply-id and running-total dedupe splits the work exactly as before, then keeps what that file counted: its quarter-hours by raw model as `TokenTally` (cache reads and writes, the one-hour share, context bands, missing cache fields), reply timings, title, directory and review mark. Kept files are priced afresh with live ones, so days, money, unpriced tokens, model detail, cache savings, hours, output speeds and session and project rows all stay as they were.

- **A kept file still claims its replies.** Its message ids and Codex running totals are claimed before any live file is read, so a copy still on disk does not count them again. **Claims are never released**: a file holding the copies can be away for a scan (a moved folder, an unmounted volume) or come back from a backup. They are stored as 64-bit digests (`ClaimDigest`, eight bytes a reply), not the per-reply records the cache holds (most of its 11 MB on one Mac, historically).
- **A kept file put back is read again**, and its kept entry, claims included, is dropped. Codex is matched by name, since it moves archived sessions into `archived_sessions` and a rollout's name carries its session id; Claude Code by path only, since it has same-named files in different folders (`journal.jsonl`).
- **Only this home's transcripts are kept.** A gone entry outside the folders the reader reads (a cache from a scan of another home) is dropped as before; a linked `~/.claude` or `~/.codex` is walked at its target and counts as inside. A reader pointed at another home without its own cache folder neither reads nor writes the archive.
- **The archive is written before the cache lets go.** If it cannot be written, or an existing file cannot be read (damaged, or from a later build), the gone entries stay in the cache and count from there, and the unreadable file is not overwritten.

**Every other agent, as a high-water mark** (`AgentArchive`, `archive-agent-<agent>.json`). These are read from whole stores into one ledger with no per-file attribution, but a past quarter-hour only grows while the readers stay the same. Each **stable** read (one `AgentCache` would persist: no reader limit, store unchanged during the read) raises the marks: per quarter-hour and raw model, kind by kind; per quarter-hour, the unclassified tokens a slot keeps without a model; per day and model, classified work with no quarter-hour (aggregate records) and unclassified tokens. What is shown is the live ledger plus whatever the marks hold beyond it, priced at today's rates through `UsageLedgerReader.price`. The cache file the read replaces is itself a stable read and raises the marks first, which catches deletions made while Pulse was not running.

- **Time zones.** Quarter-hours are keyed by instant (UTC quarter index), so a zone change moves nothing. Day-level marks remember their zone; after a change each moves to the day holding its noon and stands only where the live ledger has nothing on that day or either neighbour.
- **A copy is not counted twice.** A message a store holds in two places is folded by the reader (`deduplicationID`); deleting one copy leaves the quarter-hour as it was.
- **Sessions.** A session a stable read had and the next one lacked is kept as last read, and shown only while the work added back on its days can hold it, so a session re-filed under another live session is not listed twice. Its money is what it was priced at when kept.
- **A half-written store lowers nothing.** A read that may not be persisted shows the marks over it and changes none.
- **Not Devin Desktop** (`AgentArchive.keeps`). Its records count only where they do not mirror Devin CLI's databases, so rows the CLI deletes would reappear under Desktop; Desktop is read without an archive.
- The aggregate-timing flag is carried over only while work without an hour is being added back; both flags reset when the readers change.
- A SQLite store such as OpenCode's loses nothing to file pruning; a session deleted in the app is kept the same way, because its tokens were spent.

**Limits:**

- **Nothing deleted before Pulse read it can come back.** On upgrade the archives started from what the caches still held (the `ledger-9-*` entries of transcripts deleted since the last scan, and each agent's last stable `agent-10-*` ledger). `Recap.recordsBegin` is the first day Pulse has seen a record, which can be later than first use. **Neither cache number was bumped for the archive**, because a new number would have deleted the old file at launch and with it the only record.
- A kept transcript keeps the counts it was read under; a later parser fix reaches only files still on disk. After an `AgentCache.version` bump, marks taken under the old readers stand only for days the new readers see nothing on, since a fix may lower or re-attribute what is on disk. The trim applies to what is shown even before a stable read rewrites the archive.
- High-water marks are approximate: work added to a past quarter-hour after other work there was deleted (an import of old records, say) hides under the mark until the live figure passes it (short, never double); a record whose time a store rewrites to another quarter-hour counts at both; a session still in the store with part of its work gone keeps its row as read, so the lost part is in days, models and hours but not that row or its project; a kept session's money is not repriced.
- An agent whose store is gone entirely is not present (`SpendAgent.present`) and not read, so its kept history is hidden until a store returns.
- The archives live only in Pulse's Application Support folder; deleting it deletes them.

## Read memory and WorkBuddy

`LogLines` streams JSONL and line logs in 64 KiB reads, keeping the current line rather than a whole transcript. This bounds **IO buffering** to the chunk plus the longest line; decoded records, aggregate buckets and a single large JSON object still need memory. `AgentLogIO.jsonLines` is a lazy, replayable sequence. Claude/Codex use the same streaming path with per-line autorelease pools. Whole-file mirror hashes are computed incrementally without changing their bytes or deduplication identities.

Newline scanning uses libc's `memchr` over each chunk's contiguous bytes. A line within one chunk is an owning `Data` slice; only lines spanning chunks are assembled into a separate buffer. Slices keep their chunk alive until released, including across the next read or iterator destruction; no unsafe borrowed pointer leaves the scan. Readers still process one line at a time, with no extra concurrent full-store reads or whole-file buffers.

- WorkBuddy/CodeBuddy exclude the root-level `binaries/` subtree from **both** enumeration and fingerprinting; new or changed real logs and SQLite WALs still invalidate the cache.
- Unrelated log lines are rejected before timestamp parsing, and timestamp parsers are reused. Where transcripts supersede an extension log, only the first valid fallback record is read, which is enough to set the partial-counts flag.
- Command Code retains compact usage/ancestry fields, not conversation payloads, for branch reconciliation. CSV parsing iterates the string's scalar view rather than allocating a whole-file scalar array. Senpi/Prime input discovery reads session headers only.

## What the page will not say

- **No tokens from a cost, and no estimate as usage** ([What the readers agree on](#what-the-readers-agree-on)). Dividing spend by a rate, or a request count by anything, is a number nobody measured. Cost-only stores are still watched and can be named as recognised without a token figure; an estimate's absence is not a zero.
- **No invented kind, no silent shortfall, no invented hours** ([Unclassified, incomplete and coarse](#unclassified-incomplete-and-coarse-are-three-different-things)). A split that does not account for its total is unavailable; "Counts may be incomplete." is a known subset, never merged with an unclassified remainder or coarse timing; aggregate timing withholds the hour series.
- **Shared categories across agent products.** **Input** (`TokenTally.fresh`: plain input plus recorded cache writes), **Cache hit** and **Output**, plus **Unclassified** when a source explicitly reports tokens without usable categories — the same in all six languages, the overview, agent view, model detail and day tables. Input is the agent's recorded model input (prompts, history, tool results); it does not claim the text was new or never cached. A positive recorded write amount is named in the input note without a provider-specific billing claim; pricing keeps each kind's own rate. Output includes reasoning where its relation to output is known. Missing cache data stays unreported, and cache writes are never inferred from hits.
- **No zero for a cache nobody records.** Goose and Kiro keep no cache column (`SpendAgent.reportsCacheReads`); where every agent behind a split is one of these, the cache-hit row says **Not reported**, the model day table's cache column is blank, and the card's cache hit rate is omitted (`UsageLedger.reportsCacheReads`) rather than 0%. Per reply likewise: Claude Code pointed at a compatible endpoint can write usage with **no** cache field, counted apart from a zero (`TokenTally.repliesWithoutCacheFields`). A model none of whose replies mentioned the cache (`reportsNoCache`) is left out of the cache hit rates (the overall one covers the models that did report), and a split made only of such work says **Not reported**.
- **No span that is really the last records.** "Last 7/31 days" on the cards and the menu are calendar days back from today (`UsageLedger.recent`), not the tail of `days`, which stops at the last record and once showed the week before a break. A span with no priced token shows "—", never `$0.00`; the menu's money follows the ledger's own currency.
- **No blended-rate model estimates** ([Model amounts](#model-amounts)). The model list ranks tokens; its detail adds amounts and labels incomplete pricing. A day, month or agent whose every token is unpriced shows **no amount**; a published zero rate is a real `$0.00`.
- **No "other" bucket for sessions without a project.** Codex's own path carries no directory (and a project-less Codex Desktop chat or a DeepSeek Harness default-workspace session has only a scratch folder); such a bucket would outrank every real project. Those sessions are in every total but not on that list, and each project's bar is its share of **all** the work, so the bars add up to less than the whole.
- **No streak cut to the span, and none broken by the morning.** Current and longest streak are over every day with records, whatever the span; a run that reached yesterday stays current until a whole day passes without work.
- **No "favourite".** The caption under Pattern is **Most tokens**: models are ranked by tokens, cache reads included, which says nothing about how often one was chosen.
- **Not a bill.** The pane reads "local records and exports"; the footnote calls the figures usage records estimated at models.dev published API rates, not a bill, and a conditional line counts the models without public pricing (tokens only). Plans are subscriptions, and work on another machine appears only where it was imported.
